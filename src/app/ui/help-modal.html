How to use this app

Two practice kinds, three content sources. Pick Speaking to repeat sentences aloud, or Dictation to type what you hear.

  1. Choose a kind, a source — Topic, My text or YouTube — and how long you have, then press Start.
  2. Press Play and repeat every sentence aloud during the gap (Speaking), or type it after the recording (Dictation).
  3. Tune speed, gap and voice to your level.

What each feature is for

  • Auto Play / Pause (space) — start and stop the exercise. When paused, ← repeats the current sentence from the start.
  • Next (→) — jump ahead whenever you want.
  • Session (5/10/15 min, or unlimited) — chosen on the home screen before you start. An unlimited session runs until you press Finish; the others end on their own. Either way the app counts your time and the sentences you practiced.
  • Finish — ends the practice now and shows your summary.
  • options — hides the settings you tune once and then leave alone: the speed, gap and voice controls.
  • speed — slow it down to catch every sound, or speed it up when you feel ready.
  • gap — the pause between sentences is your time to repeat; make it longer when you need more time.
  • voice — pick the English voice that is easiest to understand (Microsoft Edge has the best selection).
  • Speaking — transcribes what you say and scores it 0–5 stars. It listens until you stop talking, and each line keeps its score. Click any sentence to have it read out and scored on demand. It needs the microphone: if you refuse the prompt the app says so and the session runs unscored.
  • Dictation — plays one sentence at a time for you to type back. Check marks it Correct or Incorrect; an incorrect check hides the words you missed behind stars until you reveal them, and Show full answer shows the whole sentence. Skip moves on without an answer.
  • Topic — the app’s own sentence lists, grouped by level.
  • My text — paste or type your own text and the app splits it into sentences you can practise like any other source. It is kept on this device only, and is read back exactly as you wrote it.
  • YouTube — paste a video link and the app plays its captions in step with the video. This works on desktop browsers with internet access; elsewhere the app reads the captions aloud with the voice instead.
  • The sentence order is shuffled every time you start a practice, so you work on the language rather than memorizing a sequence.
